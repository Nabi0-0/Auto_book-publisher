Chapter 1: The Canoe Builder

DICK stood on the coral ledge, his eyes fixed on the distant southern horizon. Behind him, the thunderous breakers of the outer sea crashed against the shore, and the wind-whipped spindrift scattered across the surface. Before him, the calm, infinite blue of the lagoon stretched out, its tranquil waters reflecting the sun's blazing heat. The lagoon of Karolin, a haven for his people, was his domain.

As he gazed out at the sea, his thoughts turned to the tribe gathered on the beach. The women, children, and youths were busy with their daily routines – fishing, playing, and working on the paraka patches. All of them were under his care. His were the canoes drawn up on the sand, and his were the empty houses where the war canoes had once rested on their rollers.

But as he turned his back on the lagoon and faced the outer beach, his brow furrowed. His mind was consumed by the memories of Palm Tree, an island shrouded in mystery, where devils lurked. Beyond the sea line, Palm Tree was a threat, a constant reminder of the danger that lurked in the shadows.

Little Tari, the son of Le Taioi, the net maker, sat nearby, watching Dick with curious eyes. Tari knew nothing of life beyond the lagoon, but he sensed that all the men of Karolin had been swept away by war, leaving the women and children vulnerable and leaderless. Then, yesterday, a strange figure had appeared, sent by the gods, it seemed, to be their chief and ruler.

The child's eyes sparkled with curiosity as he asked, "Taori, who are you?" But Dick hesitated, unsure if he could explain his story to the child. Tari's gaze was direct, unbridled by the complexities of adulthood.

As Dick gazed out at the sea, his mind began to wander, replaying the memories of his past. He recalled the island of Marua, where he had grown up with an old sailor man named Kearney. Katafa, the girl who had been blown away from Karolin, had joined them, bringing with her the language of the island. Together, they had lived a simple life, free from the dangers that now threatened their existence.

But as he stood on the coral ledge, Dick's thoughts were interrupted by the sound of children's laughter and the creaking of canoes on the beach. The tribe was gathering, and he knew he had to take action. The memory of the men from Palm Tree, who had burned the schooner and left him and Katafa for dead, still lingered, fueling his determination to protect his people.

As he gazed towards the north, Dick's mind was filled with visions of war and danger, of men building canoes and setting sail to attack the vulnerable island of Karolin. He knew that he had to prepare, to build his own canoes and train his people to defend themselves against the threats that lurked beyond the horizon.

Just then, Katafa appeared beside him, her dark hair blowing in the breeze like an eagle's feather. Her eyes, dark and luminous, fixed on the spot where Dick's gaze had wandered. "Look!" she whispered, pointing to the horizon.

As they stood there, the island of Palm Tree began to take shape, rising from the sea like a mirage. The memory of their escape, the burning of the schooner, and the horrors of the Melanesian raiders flooded back. Dick's nostrils flared, and his eyes grew dark with hatred as he recalled the threats they had made against Katafa and him.

But as they gazed out at the sea, Dick knew that he had to think beyond the immediate danger. He had to consider the wolves of civilization, the men who would stop at nothing to claim what was theirs. The foundation of all history – man's instinct for war, rapine, and destruction – was a harsh reality that he could not ignore.

As the vision of Palm Tree began to fade, Dick turned to Katafa, his eyes burning with determination. Together, they watched as the canoe approaching from the southern beach drew closer, carrying the men who would help him build the canoes of war. The fate of Karolin hung in the balance, and Dick knew that he had to act to protect his people and his island.
