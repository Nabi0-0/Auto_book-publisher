**The Gates of Morning**

**Chapter 1: The Canoe Builder**

Dick stood on the coral ledge, his eyes fixed on the south. The thunder of the outer sea's breakers and the scattered spindrift on the wind created a constant roar behind him. Before him, the lagoon of Karolin stretched out, a seemingly endless expanse of blue water, dotted with fishing gulls. The tribe bustled along the beach, their laughter and chatter filling the air.

He had arrived only yesterday, but it was his now – the canoes, the empty houses, everything. The women, children, and youths worked on the beach, fishing with nets, playing games, or tending to the paraka patches. They were all his people. Little did they know, he had a past, a life beyond this island, but it was shrouded in a haze of forgotten memories.

As he gazed out at the lagoon, his brow furrowed, and he turned his back to the water, facing the northern sea. Beyond the breakers, Palm Tree Island lay hidden, beautiful as a dream, yet infested with devils. Little Tari, the net maker's son, looked up at him from his perch on the coral. Tari knew little about life, but he knew that all the men of Karolin had been swept away in war, leaving the women and children defenseless.

Dick's arrival, in a strange boat with Katafa, the girl who had been blown away at sea, had sent a message from the gods, according to the women. Tari, alone with Dick, asked the question that had been on his mind.

"Taori, who are you?" (é kamina tai)

Dick hesitated, unsure how to answer. Would Tari understand the strange words of his story? He glanced down at the child, his mind racing with the memories of his past. The island of Palm Tree, where he had grown up with an old sailor named Kearney, came flooding back. The memories of Katafa, the girl who had lived with them and taught him the language of Karolin, were still vivid.

But he couldn't tell Tari the truth. The name "Dick" was lost in his memory, like the language of his childhood. Kearney, the sailor who had raised him, was all but forgotten, consumed by the haze of his past.

Dick's thoughts were interrupted by the sound of children hauling in a net. Tari's eyes sparkled with excitement, and he trotted off to join them. Dick turned his gaze back to the north, his mind still reeling with memories of Palm Tree and the Melanesian men who had taken over the island.

His thoughts were interrupted by Katafa's hand on his shoulder. She pointed to the north, and they stood in silence, their minds untutored, yet their eyes locked onto the island that had been their home. The vision of Palm Tree rose from the horizon, beautiful as a dream, but also a threat and a warning.

As they gazed at the island, Dick's nostrils flared, and his eyes darkened with hate. They had threatened him, threatened Katafa, and they would come again. The vision of Palm Tree seemed to repeat itself, a warning of the dangers that lay ahead. The men of Palm Tree would build canoes and come for the women of Karolin, driven by their primal instincts.

Dick knew that danger lay not only to the north but wherever greed, desire, or hatred roamed the azure sea, and that the wolves of civilization were just as deadly.
