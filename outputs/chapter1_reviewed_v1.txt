**Chapter 1: The Canoe Builder**

Dick stood atop a coral ledge, his gaze sweeping across the endless expanse of the South Pacific. The outer sea's waves crashed against the shore, sending sea spray flying in the wind. Before him, the tranquil lagoon of Karolin stretched out, its cerulean waters dotted with fishing gulls. This was his domain, claimed only yesterday.

The tribe, a bustling mix of women, children, and youths, went about their daily activities on the sun-scorched beach. They fished with nets, played games, or tended to their paraka patches. All of them were his people. His were the canoes drawn up on the sand, and his were the empty houses where the war canoes had once rested on their rollers.

As he scanned the lagoon and the houses, a furrow creased his brow. Turning his back on the lagoon, he faced the outer beach and the northern sea. Beyond the sea line, invisible, lay Palm Tree, an island as beautiful as a dream, yet teeming with danger.

Little Tari, the son of Le Taioi, the net maker, sat nearby on the coral, watching Dick with wide eyes. Tari knew little of life, but he knew that all the men of Karolin who had gone to war had left the women and children, including himself, defenseless and without a leader.

Yesterday, a mysterious new figure had arrived from the northern sea, sent by the gods, according to the women. This figure, Dick, was their new chief and ruler. Tari didn't know who the gods were, but he knew that his new leader was a mystery.

Without hesitation, Tari asked the question that had been on his mind: "Taori, who are you?" Dick's thoughts were drawn back to his past, to the life he had left behind on Palm Tree, an island that seemed a world away.

"I'm Dick Lestrange," he began, his voice carrying on the breeze. "I was left alone with an old sailor, Kearney, on Palm Tree, an island beyond sight fifty miles to the north. We lived there, and Kearney taught me to fish and build things. Then one day, Katafa, the girl who was blown away from here in a storm, joined us. She lived with us until Kearney died, and then we were alone. She taught me the language of Karolin, and we loved each other."

As he gazed out at the sea, his mind was filled with memories of the men who had attacked them on Palm Tree and the danger that still lurked beyond the horizon. The Melanesians, their enemies, would build canoes and come to Karolin, seeking to claim its women for themselves.

Dick's thoughts were interrupted by the hand on his shoulder, and he turned to face Katafa, her dark hair blown back by the breeze like an eagle's feather. Her eyes, luminous and dark, were fixed on the sea line, and she pointed to the north.

"Look!" she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

Palm Tree, their home, was visible on the horizon, a mysterious stain in the sea dazzle. As they watched, the island took shape, a beautiful dream, yet a threat and a warning. The memory of their escape, the wind in their hair, the yells of the Melanesians, all came flooding back.

Dick's nostrils expanded, and his eyes grew dark with hate. They had threatened him, and they had threatened Katafa. Some day, they would come, and he would be ready.

As they stood there, the vision of Palm Tree began to fade, and they turned to greet the canoe approaching from the southern beach. It was the canoe Dick had sent for the three old men, expert canoe builders, who would help him prepare for the danger that lay ahead.
