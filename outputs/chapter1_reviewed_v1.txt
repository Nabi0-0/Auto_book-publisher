Chapter 1: The Canoe Builder

Dick stood on the coral ledge, his gaze fixed on the south as the thunderous breakers of the outer sea crashed behind him and the wind scattered spindrift across the lagoon. Before him, the calm ocean stretched out, infinite and blue, dotted with fishing gulls. This was the lagoon of Karolin, his new home.

As he gazed out at the sea, Dick's thoughts turned to the island he had left behind, Palm Tree. The memories of the war, chaos, and destruction still lingered, and he couldn't help but feel a sense of unease. He had escaped with Katafa, the girl who had been blown back to the island years ago, and the weight of their shared past hung heavy on his mind.

Little Tari, the son of the net maker, Le Taioi, looked up at Dick with curious eyes. The boy knew little of life, but he knew that all the men of Karolin had been swept away in war, leaving the women and children defenseless. Dick, the stranger, had arrived yesterday, sent by the gods, or so the women believed, to be their leader and chief.

Tari's eyes sparkled with curiosity as he asked Dick, "Who are you?" Dick's thoughts stumbled, lost in the foggy memory of his past. He had grown up on Palm Tree, raised by Kearney, a sailor who had taught him to fish and build little ships. But the memories were hazy, like the past itself.

Katafa, who had been watching from behind, stepped forward, her dark hair blowing back in the breeze like an eagle's feather. Her eyes, luminous and dark, met Dick's, and she pointed to the north. "Look!" she said.

Palm Tree, the island they had escaped from, rose from the sea, a mirage born of the heat and haze. Dick and Katafa stood transfixed, their minds untutored by the ways of the world, their eyes fixed on the place where their nightmare had begun.

As they gazed out at the island, memories flooded back – the Melanesian raid, the burning of the schooner, and the desperate escape in the dinghy. The pain and fear still lingered, and Dick's eyes grew dark with a mixture of emotions. They had threatened him, and they had threatened Katafa, his love.

Katafa's hand brushed against his, and he felt a sense of calm wash over him. They would build canoes, and when they did, they would be ready. The vision of Palm Tree seemed to echo through Dick's mind, a warning and a reminder of the dangers that lay ahead.

As the vision faded, Katafa pointed to a canoe approaching from the southern beach. It was the one Dick had sent to fetch the old canoe builders. Together, they walked down to the inner beach to meet it, their hearts heavy with the weight of their past and the uncertain future.
