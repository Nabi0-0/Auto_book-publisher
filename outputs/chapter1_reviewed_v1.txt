CHAPTER I: THE CANOE BUILDER

Dick stood on the coral ledge, his gaze fixed on the southern horizon. Behind him, the waves of the outer sea crashed against the shore, and the sea spray danced on the wind. Before him, the tranquil lagoon of Karolin stretched out, a vast expanse of blue water dotted with fishing birds. It was his, he who had arrived just yesterday, and it was his people – the women, children, and youths busily working along the beach, playing, or tending to their paraka patches.

As he scanned the beach, his brow furrowed, and he turned his back to the lagoon, facing the breakers on the outer beach and the northern sea. Beyond the horizon, Palm Tree, an island of both beauty and danger, lay shrouded in mystery, its dark history of devils and threats still fresh in his mind.

Little Tari, the son of Le Taioi, the net maker, gazed up at him from the coral rocks. Tari knew little about life, but he knew that all the men of Karolin who had gone to war had left the women and children, including himself, defenseless and leaderless. Yesterday, a new figure had arrived from the northern sea, in a strange boat, with Katafa, the girl who had been swept away from this shore years ago. The women said she was sent by the gods to be their chief and ruler.

As the child stood before him, Dick couldn't help but feel a sense of responsibility wash over him. "Who are you?" Tari asked, his curiosity piqued.

Dick hesitated, unsure how to respond. "I am Taori," he said finally, his voice low and measured. "Why do you ask?"

Tari shrugged. "I just ask as I breathe," he replied, his eyes sparkling with mischief. "But no big folk will ever answer my questions."

Dick's gaze drifted back to the north, his mind consumed by the memories of the past. He saw again the schooner that had arrived at Palm Tree, only to be burned by the Melanesian men, and he saw again Katafa and himself making their escape in the old dinghy. He saw their landing on this beach, yesterday, and the women and children swarming around him, considering him sent by the gods to be their chief and leader.

As he gazed towards the north, the memory of the men who had driven them away stained the beauty of the sea and sky. There was no immediate danger, but he knew they would come, and he would have to prepare.

Dick was a man with a past shrouded in mystery, but he knew he could think forward, imagine, and plan. That's why he had sent a canoe that morning to fetch three old canoe builders, and why his eyes had brightened at the sight of nearly a hundred youths ripening to war age.

But beneath it all, lighting his mind, raising his daring to eagle heights, lay his passion for Katafa, his other self, more dear to him than life itself. As he turned to face her, he saw her dark hair blowing back like an eagle's feather, her eyes fixed on the sea line. "Look!" she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

Palm Tree, though far behind the sea line, began to appear through the mirage, a beautiful, diaphanous vision. They saw again the savages on the beach, the wind in the dinghy's sail, and the yells of the Melanesians. And as they gazed, Dick's nostrils expanded, and his eyes grew dark with hate. They had threatened him, and they had threatened Katafa, and they still threatened her.

Some day, they would come, and Dick knew it. They would build canoes and see the lagoon light in the sky, and they would come. Ah, if he had known, danger lay not only to the north, but wherever greed or desire or hatred might roam on that azure sea, not only amongst savages, but the wolves of civilization.

But for now, he focused on the canoe approaching from the southern beach, bearing the canoe builders he had sent for. Leaving the coral, they made their way down to the white sand of the inner beach to meet it.
