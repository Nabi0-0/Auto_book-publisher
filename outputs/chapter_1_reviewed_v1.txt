CHAPTER I: THE CANOE BUILDER

Dick stood on the coral ledge, his eyes fixed on the horizon. Behind him, the thunderous breakers of the outer sea crashed against the wind, while before him stretched a serene lagoon, an ocean-like expanse of infinite blue, scattered with fishing gulls. This was the lagoon of Karolin, and it was his. He had arrived here only yesterday, and the entire tribe, women, children, and youths, were his people.

As he scanned the beach, busy with fishing nets, games, and patchwork, his gaze landed on the canoes drawn up on the sand and the empty houses where war canoes had once rested on their rollers. His brow furrowed, and he turned away from the lagoon, facing the breakers on the outer beach and the northern sea. Beyond the sea line, invisible, lay Palm Tree, an island swarming with devils.

Little Tari, the son of Le Taioi, the net maker, sat nearby, gazing up at Dick with curiosity. Tari knew little of life, but he understood that all the men of Karolin had been swept away by war, leaving the women and children defenseless and without a leader.

Dick, a stranger with a mysterious past, had arrived with Katafa, the girl who had been blown away from the island years ago. The women believed he was sent by the gods to be their chief and ruler. Tari, unaware of the gods, asked the question that had been on his mind: "Taori, who are you?" Dick hesitated, unsure if he could explain his true identity.

As he gazed out at the sea, his mind wandered back to the events that had led him here. Katafa had joined him, and they lived together until Kearney's death. Then, they were alone, and Katafa taught him the language of Karolin, naming him Taori. They loved each other, and Dick felt a pang of loss, realizing that all his memories before arriving at Karolin were fading.

Suddenly, a thought struck him: war canoes. Would he defend against invaders in the lagoon or attack them before they reached the shore? The uncertainty gnawed at him, but his thoughts were interrupted by Katafa's hand on his shoulder.

"Look!" she said, pointing to the northern sea line. Palm Tree, their homeland, was reappearing through mirage, its beauty and danger rising like a specter from the past.

Dick and Katafa stood shoulder to shoulder, their minds struggling to comprehend the phenomenon, yet drawn to the distant island. They saw the savages on the beach, the dinghy racing for safety, and the yells of the Melanesians. The memory of their escape and the danger that still threatened them was palpable.

As they gazed at the island, Dick's nostrils expanded, and his eyes darkened with a sense of foreboding. The Melanesians had threatened him and Katafa, and they still threatened her. He knew that one day they would return, building canoes to reach Karolin, and he felt an overwhelming desire to protect the women and children.

The vision of Palm Tree faded, and they turned to the lagoon, where a canoe was approaching from the southern beach. Dick recognized it as the one he had sent for the old canoe builders, Aioma, Palia, and Tafata. As they walked down to the inner beach to meet it, Dick's thoughts were consumed by the uncertainty of their future and the danger that lurked beyond the horizon.
